# Mathematical and statistical review of the bounded dW branch

Reviewed the fixed package `.superpowers/dw_sampling/review-package-fixed.txt`, SHA256 `5b5dd44ae99e76467c48d361b922a5e978b785253ef07595ced52e4b82c348fd`. At the initial hash check, the six source/config/scope files matched the SHA256 values embedded in that package. Locations below refer to those frozen versions; concurrent live-source changes are outside this review. This was a read-only source review, apart from writing this review: no tests, device operations, benchmarks, corpus access, or source changes. The reported 113 passing CPU tests are supplied evidence, not independently rerun evidence here. Backend readiness is a separate review.

**Conditional conclusion:** I found no blocking algebraic defect in the specified estimator, reference enumeration, A/A threshold calculation, or paired log-ratio calculation. The code supports proceeding only through the existing prerequisite gates. It supplies no measurement, learning-quality, optimizer-equivalence-to-dense, or time-to-target result. The following limits must survive into any scientific report.

1. **Timing intervals are conditional block-level intervals, with unverified dependence assumptions.** `analysis/run_dw_sampling.py:161–170` correctly computes `exp(mean(log(candidate/dense)))` and resamples entire paired log ratios, using 10,000 draws, seed 62001, and linear 2.5/97.5 percentiles. It preserves within-pair dependence. However, independent resampling of the 30 pairs assumes their empirical distribution adequately represents repeated block timing; it does not preserve serial dependence between successive pairs. `analysis/run_dw_sampling.py:601–615` randomizes arm order, which reduces order confounding but does not establish independence or remove thermal/frequency drift across pairs. Two sessions and the A/A margin are useful safeguards, not a proof of nominal coverage under such dependence. Report the raw time/order series and adverse drift if present; describe these intervals as the prescribed block-bootstrap screen. Do not turn a pass into a general 95% learning or machine-population guarantee. The upper endpoint is the 97.5th percentile of a two-sided 95% interval, as implemented, rather than a separately computed one-sided 95th percentile.

2. **The eligibility search has no simultaneous-confidence correction.** `analysis/run_dw_sampling.py:927–936` can pass cost on any of six sampler/rho arms at either of two middle shapes, provided the same arm/shape passes both sessions. This correctly enforces two-session intersection for each eligible entry and excludes wide-only success. The twelve searches share observations/conditions and are not jointly calibrated by the marginal bootstrap intervals. Requiring both sessions does not, without further assumptions, produce a quantified familywise error bound. This is an explicitly bounded screening decision, not confirmatory inference about a selected winner. Preserve all arm/shape results and the exact eligible entries; uniform-only eligibility must remain a uniform-only contingency. Any later positive language-model claim still requires the separate frozen seed-level confirmation in `decisions/dw_sampling_scope.md:162–173`. No post hoc multiplicity fix or changed gate is proposed here.

3. **The timing repetitions do not independently sample learning trajectories.** Each measured block restores `common`, including generator states (`analysis/run_dw_sampling.py:583–609`; `Fixture.restore` at lines 429–441). The candidate sampling states are inserted into that common snapshot at lines 715–719. Thus all 30 timed blocks in one comparison replay the same initial parameters, native moments, data, and sampler sequence. This follows the prospective reset rule and is appropriate for repeatable conditional cost measurement. The 30 blocks estimate timing variation for that trajectory; they are neither 30 independent sampler-realization experiments nor 30 training seeds. Between sessions the named base seed changes. The report must preserve this distinction. The setup charge in lines 616–624 is amortized over exactly `30 * updates_per_block` updates; it is a declared finite-horizon cost convention, not a measured full-run launch cost.

4. **A/A delta is a frozen empirical margin, not a confidence limit.** `analysis/run_dw_sampling.py:173–178` implements `max(.05, 2 * linear_quantile95(abs(TA/TB - 1)))`; lines 897–910 pool all five shapes across both retained sessions and freeze the result for D. This matches the specified rule, including the deterministic first-session replacement when the pooled value exceeds .15 and the replacement allowance remains. Pooling describes the mixture of these shapes and does not establish a per-shape noise bound. The native-only provenance, invalid/replacement attempts, and every shape's raw A/A results must remain visible; delta should not be reported as a confidence interval or universal measurement error. No sampler timings are used to choose delta.

5. **Unbiased raw dW does not mean unbiased clipped gradients, AdamW updates, or equivalent future trajectories.** `src/oar/sampled_linear.py:171–195` computes raw dX and db without a sampled adjoint. With fixed weights throughout the effective backward, this also preserves upstream adjoints. Native clipping subsequently uses the norm of the complete gradient, including sampled dW (`analysis/run_dw_sampling.py:414–421`), so its random scaling can affect even an otherwise exact bias/unsampled gradient. AdamW is nonlinear in the gradients and moment history. The injected-sample multistep test (`tests/test_sampled_linear.py:324–351`) establishes equivalence to native AdamW given the same supplied gradients; it does not establish equivalence to dense-gradient AdamW. The counterexample at lines 354–364 correctly makes that distinction. Likewise, the zero-dW arm retains moment/decay work but changes the trajectory and clipping factor; its feasibility result is an optimistic nonlearning diagnostic, not a universal runtime lower bound.

The estimator itself has the correct mathematical structure. Put `A_i = outer(D_i, X_i)`, `G = sum_i A_i`, and condition on the current operands and resulting positive probabilities. For iid indices with replacement,

`Ghat = (1/k) * sum_j A_{I_j}/p_{I_j}`

has `E[Ghat | X,D] = G`, with

`E[||Ghat-G||_F^2 | X,D] = (sum_i ||A_i||_F^2/p_i - ||G||_F^2)/k`.

There is no extra division by n. Duplicate rows contribute with their multiplicities. Splitting each inverse factor into two `1/sqrt(k*p_i)` factors before the small GEMM implements the same expression (`src/oar/sampled_linear.py:121–131`). Uniform's direct-randint path and `n/k` scaling are also correct. Native dispatch for k>=n avoids claiming that a with-replacement k=n estimator is exact (`src/oar/sampled_linear.py:229–234`). The proof remains valid for approximate norm scores, provided the same positive distribution drives both sampling and weighting; it does not require perfectly accurate scores or optimal importance probabilities.

The exhaustive n/k cases use an independent FP64 probability reference, all ordered sample sequences, the actual gather/weight/GEMM path, and the coordinatewise variance identity (`tests/test_sampled_linear.py:34–59`). The independent outer-product comparison at lines 397–408 and hand-computed mixture at lines 387–391 complement enumeration. Unequal microbatch weighting and local draws are covered at lines 269–288 and 411–423. These are meaningful deterministic checks of the stated mathematics; they do not by themselves qualify MPS rounding or runtime behavior.

Numerical qualifications remain material. `src/oar/sampled_linear.py:85–102` checks both original operand scales, returns zero only when an entire original operand is zero, rejects zero/invalid score mass for nonzero operands, and maintains the uniform floor. Global scaling can lose individual FP32 score contributions, but those rows retain positive mixture mass. The floor bounds inverse importance weights; it does not bound relative gradient error when outer products cancel and `||G||_F` is small. FP32 cast factors, operand scaling, and GEMM rounding mean exact real-arithmetic unbiasedness is not literal bitwise floating-point unbiasedness; the casting/reference tests supply specified fixture tolerances, not a universal error theorem. The offline formula at `analysis/run_dw_sampling.py:789–803` is the ideal conditional Frobenius variance for its actual probabilities, while realized MPS errors additionally contain arithmetic error. The 32 one-step draws at native states 0/32/128 are descriptive diagnostics, not evidence of long-horizon loss behavior.
